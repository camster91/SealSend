# SealSend growth and SEO strategy
Prepared October 10, 2026. Initial working audience: clubs and community organizers, subject to Cameron's preference. This is a plan, not proof of traction or ranking. No ads purchased, outreach sent, new analytics service installed or Search Console access assumed.

## The first promise
Create a digital invitation, collect replies and welcome guests from one simple workflow. Lead with the gathering and the headcount problem. Free beta: one active event, up to 100 guests. Do not market unlimited recurring events, paid tickets, SMS or offline check-in. The beta supports trying one event; willingness to return is a question to measure, not an entitlement promise.

## Acquisition before scale
Recruit a small set of actual organizers through Cameron's existing relationships. Offer help setting up their first event and watch their invitation, RSVP and check-in journey with permission. Learn why a host would choose this over a group chat or an established invitation app. Start with 10 hosts and 5 published real events as experiment targets, not revenue forecasts. No unapproved external messages.

One event can introduce SealSend to guests through the existing Powered by badge. Review that path as a voluntary invitation to become a host after replying. Do not interrupt RSVPs or add guests to marketing lists. Count new hosts attributable to this path rather than treating every guest visit as a potential customer.

## Search foundation observed live
Home, robots.txt, sitemap.xml and clubs page return200. Home and clubs page have correct canonicals and no noindex. Sitemap lists14 marketing/legal URLs. Seven audience pages exist. Source has activation milestones for account_created, event_published, first_rsvp_received, first_guest_checked_in and event_repeated. These are instrumentation capabilities, not current funnel totals.

The available search lookup did not surface SealSend results. That is not proof of Google exclusion: no authenticated Search Console coverage, clicks or impressions were available. Verify the domain in Google Search Console, submit sitemap.xml and inspect home plus the two new feature pages after release. Prefer HTML verification if available; DNS changes require separate authorization. Check Bing Webmaster Tools afterward.

## Content priorities
1. Strengthen existing clubs/community pages with actual workflow examples and permitted host evidence, keeping one page per intent.
2. New /rsvp-tracking explains replies, plus-ones, dietary notes and beta limits.
3. New /qr-event-check-in explains arrivals, manual fallback and device/connection requirements.
4. Publish one practical guide per week for four weeks, starting with a club-dinner RSVP checklist and an editable reminder message. Each guide should answer the question fully before its CTA.
5. Add a real host case study only after permission and measured results. Comparison pages follow direct experience and dated competitor verification; do not invent switched customers, rankings or reviews.

## Measurement
Track the funnel from channel/landing page to account creation, event publication and first RSVP. Review cohorts rather than raw page views. Current channel attribution persistence has not been demonstrated: tagged URLs alone do not prove attribution. Specify and verify first-party source capture before relying on it; do not store guest names, invitation tokens, private URLs or email addresses in analytics. Keep guest participation out of automatic marketing enrollment.

Weekly scorecard: qualified host signups, published events, events receiving a first RSVP within7 days, median publish time, support minutes per activated host, returning-host intent/use, and channel cost per activated host. Show numerator/denominator and unknown baselines. Review Search Console impressions, clicks and indexed marketing URLs monthly. Do not optimize for domain-authority scores.

## Experiment targets and decisions
Weeks1–4: recruit10 consenting hosts; target5 published events and4 of those receiving a real RSVP; record friction and support time. If hosts cannot publish or get replies, fix onboarding before expanding acquisition.
Days31–90: aim for20 hosts with an event and a first RSVP; publish4 useful guides and2 approved case studies if evidence exists. These are adjustable operating targets. Traffic, rankings, search volumes, conversion rates and acquisition costs are unknown; set baselines first. No forecast promises.

## Trust and technical work
Preserve responsive accessible pages, unique descriptive titles, self-canonicals, crawlable internal links and a marketing-only sitemap. Add truthful structured data only where it describes visible content; no fabricated ratings or FAQ-rich-result promise. Check field Core Web Vitals through Search Console when data exists; this pass did not measure field performance.

Privacy finding to resolve before wider discovery: src/app/e/[slug]/page.tsx currently inherits indexable metadata and includes event descriptions/social images; robots does not disallow /e/. Treat invitation URLs, especially tokenized links, as private sharing surfaces and review noindex defaults, canonicals and all guest/client pages. No actual guest URLs were crawled in this review. Robots exclusions alone are not access control or guaranteed removal. Do not add event pages to the marketing sitemap.

## Resourcing
Default cash budget is$0; allow roughly4–6 hours per week for host onboarding, one useful article and measurement. No ad spend is authorized. After activation and repeat-use signals exist, propose a capped paid-search experiment around one landing page, with budget and stop conditions approved first. SEO is an accumulating channel; direct relationships supply the first users while organic discovery develops.
