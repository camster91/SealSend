# First ten-host pilot

Working audience: organizers of small clubs, neighbourhood dinners and volunteer groups with a real gathering in the next2–4 weeks. Ask people Cameron already knows first. Do not buy lists or send this without selecting recipients and authorizing the message.

## Invitation draft — unsent
Hi [name], I’m testing SealSend, a digital invitation and RSVP app for small gatherings. You create an invitation, share one link, and see everyone’s replies in one guest list. Guests don’t need an account. Would you like help trying it for your next [club dinner/gathering]? The beta is free for one active event with up to100 guests. I’d appreciate hearing what felt easy and what got in your way. Here’s the site: https://sealsend.app

## Trial session
Ask how they collect replies now, what they last got wrong, and when their next real event happens. Help them create an invitation without doing every task for them. Ask permission before observing or recording. Confirm a real invitation reaches an approved inbox. Have the host preview and choose whether to share it with their guests. Do not contact guests on the host’s behalf without explicit instructions.

After the event, ask: Did this save you effort? Which step needed help? Would you use it for another event? What would you use instead? What would make it worth paying for? Record support time and real outcomes rather than flattering answers. Ask separately for permission to publish any quotation or case study.

## Scorecard
Host recruited → account created → event published → first real RSVP → event completed → willingness to return. Target10 recruited,5 published,4 with a first RSVP initially; these are experiment goals, not observed results. Identify every drop-off and its reason. Real customer names and contacts belong in an authorized private operational record, not repository docs or research prompts.
